Long-term plan

Priorities

What guides the long-term plan, beyond any single term in office, framed against the same development goals the international community already tracks, because accountability shouldn't need translation.

Illustrative photo
Priority 01

Healthcare

Every ward with reliable access to a functioning clinic: not just outreach camps, but permanent care residents can count on.

UN SDG 3 Good Health and Well-Being

Embakasi South's healthcare priority, permanent clinics rather than outreach camps alone, sits inside this same global goal.

How we'll know it's working

Hospitals stocked with medication, sub-county hospitals upgraded, ambulances acquired and Community Health Workers paid, each logged on the Development Tracker as it happens.

See the Health promises on the Scorecard
Illustrative photo
Priority 02

Education

Schools, bursaries, and digital literacy that give every child in Embakasi South a real shot, not just a nearby classroom.

UN SDG 4 Quality Education

Schools, bursaries, and digital literacy for every child here map directly onto this target.

How we'll know it's working

ECDE classrooms built, teachers employed on permanent terms and paid on time, and every bursary allocation published for anyone to check.

See the Education promises on the Scorecard
Illustrative photo
Priority 03

Housing & Infrastructure

Upgraded settlements, roads that hold up through a rainy season, and drainage that doesn't flood the same streets every year.

UN SDG 9 & 11 Infrastructure & Sustainable Communities

Roads, drainage, and upgraded settlements: the same standard the UN sets for cities worldwide, applied ward by ward.

How we'll know it's working

Roads improved, street lights installed and police posts built, each logged with its date, budget and status.

See the Infrastructure promises on the Scorecard
Illustrative photo
Priority 04

Youth & Employment

Jobs and enterprise support that don't require leaving the constituency to find real opportunity.

UN SDG 8 Decent Work and Economic Growth

Jobs and enterprise support that keep opportunity inside the constituency, not just passing through it.

How we'll know it's working

Women and youth enrolled in empowerment programmes, with participation and results reported publicly.

See the Youth & Employment promises on the Scorecard

Plans It Connects To

The UN goals frame the vision for the world. These are the plans that actually govern Embakasi South, from the nation down to the constituency.

  1. 01National

    Kenya Vision 2030

    Kenya's national long-term development blueprint, which runs to 2030.

    How our priorities connect

    These priorities sit in the areas Vision 2030 sets for the country: education, health, water and sanitation, housing, infrastructure and opportunity for young people. Locally, the Development Tracker shows how each one is delivered.

  2. 02County

    Nairobi City County Integrated Development Plan

    The five year development plan that county governments prepare, setting the county's priorities and budgets.

    How our priorities connect

    The county's plan sets what the county will deliver. The foundation's record shows what actually reaches Embakasi South, ward by ward, so residents can compare the two.

  3. 03Constituency

    NG-CDF

    The National Government Constituencies Development Fund, which finances development projects in each constituency. It is one of the funding sources shown on the Development Tracker.

    How our priorities connect

    Projects funded through NG-CDF appear on the Development Tracker with their funding source, so residents can see what the fund delivers in each ward.

The Horizon

Three phases, from a verified baseline to a record built to outlast any one term.

  1. On the ground, Embakasi South
    Phase 1 2026 to 2027

    Verified Baseline

    Every ward's current infrastructure, health, education, and water access status documented and published before the 2027 election.

  2. Illustrative photo
    Phase 2 2027 to 2032

    Delivery Under Watch

    Every project promised during the term tracked to completion, or explained, in public, in real time.

  3. Illustrative photo
    Phase 3 2032 and beyond

    A Standing Record

    Embakasi South's permanent, independent development record, open to residents, researchers, and partners alike, regardless of who holds the seat.

Questions

Straight answers to what people ask most.

Is this a campaign document?

Erick Janganya is an independent candidate for the Embakasi South seat in the 2027 general election, and he founded the Erick Janganya Foundation. The foundation's job is a public, checkable record of what is promised and delivered in Embakasi South, and Erick holds himself to the same standard: his own promises are on the Promise Scorecard for anyone to check.

How does this relate to the tracker?

The vision is the long-term plan. The Development Tracker records the projects actually happening, dated and budgeted, and the Promise Scorecard tracks each commitment against what has been delivered. Together they let anyone compare what was promised with what was done.

Where do the promises come from?

The Promise Scorecard lists the commitments from Erick's 2022 campaign platform (#KaziKwaMpango). Because he has not yet held the seat, none of them can show progress yet, and each is marked Not started.

How is the work funded?

Every project on the Development Tracker is labelled with its funding source: Foundation-funded, Government (NG-CDF), or Partner-funded. The foundation holds itself to the same standard, publishing where its own funding comes from and how it is spent.

Who can partner with the foundation?

Diaspora groups, development organisations, and mission-aligned investors who want to back real, ward-level work. Start a conversation.

Can residents shape the priorities?

Yes. Use the form above to tell us what is missing for your ward. Suggestions go to the foundation team.
